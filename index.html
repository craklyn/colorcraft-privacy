Sketchino — Privacy Policy

Last updated: 1 November 2026

The short version

Sketchino (called ColorCraft until October 2026) turns what a child types, says, or photographs into a black-and-white colouring page. To do that, the words and any photo they choose are sent to an AI service that draws the picture, and the picture comes back.

We do not ask for a name, an email address, or an account. We do not show ads. We do not use analytics or tracking of any kind. We do not sell anything about anyone, ever.

The one thing worth knowing before handing the app to a child: photos are off until a grown-up switches them on. When they are on, a photo the child takes or picks is uploaded to our service and passed to OpenAI to be turned into a drawing. It is not stored by us. Switching photos on — on the first screen the app shows a grown-up, or later on the grown-ups screen — asks a grown-ups question, once; after that the child can use photos without asking again, until a grown-up switches them off. If that is not something you want, leave photos off — everything else works from typed or spoken words.

We also keep a record of how each drawing session went, so we can see where the app helps and where it gets in the way: the broad category asked for (animals, space, vehicles and so on, chosen from a fixed list in our code), what kind of step each request was, whether each one worked, and the date. There is no time of day and nothing written by a child — the category comes from a fixed list, so it cannot contain a name or a place.

These records are not linked to an installation. They are labelled with a random session number, so they describe a drawing session rather than a person, and there is no way for us to tell which sessions belong to the same child.

What we do not collect

No name, email address, phone number, postal address, date of birth, or account of any kind. No contacts, no location, no advertising identifier. No analytics, no crash reporting, no third-party trackers or SDKs for either. No behavioural profiling and no advertising, so nothing here is used to target anyone.

What leaves the device, and where it goes

Typed words and chosen photos. These go to our service, which passes them to OpenAI to generate the picture. We do not store either — once the picture has been made, nothing of the request remains on our side. The words are used once, in passing, to pick the broad category described above — the words themselves are then gone. OpenAI processes them under its own API policy and may retain content briefly for safety and abuse monitoring before deleting it.

Spoken words, only if the microphone button is used. Speech is turned into text by Apple (on iOS) or Google (on Android) using the device's own dictation service. The audio is never recorded — not by us and not by the app. Only the transcript remains, and from then on it is treated exactly like typed words: sent in passing to make the picture, not stored by us, and kept on the device as part of the session's history. One extra step: before drawing, the transcript goes through our service to OpenAI once more, to be tidied into one short sentence (dropping the "um"s and repeats). That tidy-up is not stored either, and if it fails the child's own words are drawn as they were.

Free Build pictures. Build's word tiles bring up pictures from a free, shared library, one picture for every combination of tiles. We don't record who picked which tiles. We don't keep the spoken words, typed words or photos your child uses to make their own pictures.

A device check, via Apple App Attest (iOS) or Google Play Integrity (Android). It confirms the app is genuine and running on a real device — a yes/no answer carrying no information about you. It exists so free pictures cannot be farmed by automated installs.

Nothing else leaves the device for us or for anyone we choose. Your own device's backup is a separate matter, described below — it goes to your family's own account, not to us.

Sharing and printing. A finished page has a share button and a print button, and a child can use both without a grown-up. Share opens the device's own share sheet, so a page goes only where the person holding the device sends it — a message, an email, a photo album — and never to us. A page made from a photo is a drawing of that photo, and it is meant to look like the people in it, so a shared page can show a recognisable drawing of a child. If that matters to you, keep photos off, or keep an eye on what gets shared.

What stays on the device, and what your device backs up

Finished pictures and the history of every session live on the device. Doodles drawn on the waiting screen are never saved at all — they last only as long as the app stays open.

If the device backs itself up (Google's backup on Android, iCloud on an iPhone or iPad — a setting the device's owner controls, not us), Sketchino chooses carefully what may be included:

- Always: the words behind every picture — what was asked for, each picture's title, and how each session unfolded — the small record that proves this installation owns its remaining pictures, and the grown-up's choices (whether photos are on, picture detail and style). - As space allows: the newest finished colouring pages — roughly the most recent few hundred. - Never: photos you chose, the working images that pictures are refined from, and grayscale or colour pictures. Those exist only on the device.

That backup belongs to your family's own Google or Apple account. It is never sent to us and we cannot read it. It exists so that a lost, broken or replaced tablet does not take a family's pictures — or their remaining picture count — with it. Backups run when the device decides, typically while charging on Wi-Fi, so a picture made moments before a tablet is lost may not have been saved yet. Setting up a new device directly from the old one may carry everything, including the things cloud backup leaves out.

Deleting the app removes everything it stored on the device. A backup may remain in your family's account: on Android, reinstalling the app can bring those pictures back — usually a happy surprise after an accidental delete — while on an iPhone or iPad the backup returns only when the whole device is restored or migrated. To remove one page, press and hold it in My pages, answer the grown-ups question, and choose Delete page: that page goes from the device at once and from the backup at the next backup pass. Pages that were made by changing it are kept. When you want everything gone for good, the grown-ups screen (behind the lock on the home screen) has Erase all pages: it removes every picture and session from the device immediately, and the device's backup is replaced with an empty one the next time the system runs a backup pass. Pictures remaining are kept, so erasing a child's gallery never costs a family what they paid for.

Children

Sketchino is made for families: for children, and for the grown-ups alongside them. The way we most hope it is used is a parent and child making pictures together — but children will sometimes use it on their own, including children too young to read, so the whole app is built to the standard that audience deserves. A child cannot hand over personal information: there is nothing to sign up for and nothing to fill in. The app never asks a child for their name, age, or any other detail about themselves. What a child types or says passes through our service only to make the picture, and is not stored there.

Everything that affects money or a grown-up's decisions sits behind a lock on the home screen, and the lock asks a simple arithmetic question intended to be answered by an adult: buying pictures, the grown-ups screen and its choices, this policy and the contact address, and deleting pages. Switching photos on asks the same question, because a photo is the one thing that leaves the device as content; switching them off asks nothing. The question is a speed bump for small children, not a check of anyone's age or identity.

The installation record above exists to run the service and prevent abuse — authenticating the installation, tracking remaining pictures, and stopping the free allowance being farmed. It is not used to build a profile, to advertise, or for any purpose beyond operating the app.

A parent should know three things. First, photos: they are off until you switch them on. Once on, a photo the child chooses leaves the device to be turned into a drawing — the only content that ever goes to us, and it is not stored — and the child does not need to ask you each time. Second, sharing: a child can send a finished page through the device's share sheet without asking, and a page made from a photo can look like the people in it. Third, backup: if the device's own backup is switched on, a child's words and newest colouring pages are kept in your family's own Google or Apple account, as described above — never in ours, apart from the shared free Build pictures above. Photos can be left off, and the device's backup is yours to switch off.

Keeping and deleting data

The installation record is kept while the installation is in use, because it holds the remaining picture count. If an installation goes unused for two years, its record — including any unused pictures — is deleted automatically. Deleting the app removes everything stored on the device; the small server-side record remains until removed on request or by that two-year timeline. Free Build pictures belong to no installation's record, so they are kept while the service runs and are not removed when a record is deleted. The grown-ups screen's Erase all pages removes all pictures and sessions from the device and, at the device's next backup pass, from its backup.

To have an installation record deleted, send us the support code shown on the grown-ups screen and we will delete it. We cannot find a record without that code, because we hold nothing else that connects it to anyone.
